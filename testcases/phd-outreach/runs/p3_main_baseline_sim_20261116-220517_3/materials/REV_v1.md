**Statement of Purpose: PhD in Computer Science (Human-Computer Interaction), Khoury College**

For the past year I've been an undergraduate research assistant in the Social Computing Lab at Brookfield, helping with a PhD student's interview study about how teens use online communities for mental-health support. Most of what I did was support work. I sent recruitment messages, ran the screener, transcribed and de-identified interviews, and later helped code some of them. I didn't design the study or lead any part of it. But transcribing meant listening to every interview closely, sometimes several times, and one of them has stayed with me.

The participant talked about an online server that was one of the only places they felt they could say they were having a bad day. Then the moderators added a bot that automatically deleted posts with certain words, and some of their posts just disappeared. I get why the moderators did it. They were probably trying to keep the space safe. But I keep wondering who those tools are actually for, and what it does to someone when the place they go for help starts quietly removing what they say.

I think part of the reason it stuck with me is that I'd run into a version of this before without really noticing it. In a machine learning class, my team built a model to classify the sentiment of posts from Reddit support communities. I did the error analysis. The model kept labeling sarcastic or dark-humor posts as negative, even though a lot of them were just people coping the way people do. At the time I saw it as an accuracy problem. Now I wonder what it means when tools like that decide what stays up.

So the question I keep coming back to is roughly this: how does automated moderation change what teens feel okay saying when they're looking for help? I'm honestly still unsure what the right research question is, or how I'd study it. That's a big part of why I want to do a PhD. I've seen how a study works from the inside, mostly from the assistant's chair. Now I want to learn how to ask a question myself and follow it through.
