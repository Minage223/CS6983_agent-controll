I'm applying for the Data Analyst (New Grad, 2027) role. I'm a senior at Brookfield University studying Computer Science with a minor in Data Science, and I graduate in May 2027. I've spent the last year working with data in two settings. As an intern I built dashboards, and as a research assistant I worked with interview data from teens. Last summer I was a Data Analyst Intern at Brightpath Learning, an ed-tech company. I built a Tableau dashboard that tracked weekly active students, lessons completed, and learning streaks for the product team. I also wrote the SQL for the tables behind it. The hardest part was the data itself. When the app went to the background, it split one session into several, so sessions were being double-counted. I redefined a session as activity with gaps of less than 30 minutes, using window functions over the event timestamps. After that fix, the numbers matched how students actually used the app. My manager found the breakdown of streaks by grade level especially useful.

Since October 2025, I've been an undergraduate research assistant in Brookfield's Social Computing Lab. I work on a PhD-student-led interview study about how teens aged 15–17 use online communities for mental-health support. I sent recruitment messages and ran the eligibility screener. I transcribed and de-identified 14 interviews, and I coded 6 transcripts with the team's codebook. In our inter-rater reliability check we reached a Cohen's kappa of 0.71 after one round of reconciliation. This work taught me to handle sensitive youth data carefully, and it gave me hands-on practice with the qualitative coding your survey work involves.

In a team machine learning project, we fine-tuned a transformer to classify sentiment in Reddit support posts. It reached a macro-F1 of 0.81, compared with 0.72 for a TF-IDF baseline. I led the error analysis. I found that the model often labeled sarcastic or dark-humor posts as negative. That was a good reminder that the numbers only mean something once you understand the people behind them.

I'd be glad to bring SQL, Python, Tableau, and qualitative research skills to a Pinecrest client team, and to learn from your mentorship program. Thank you for your consideration.
