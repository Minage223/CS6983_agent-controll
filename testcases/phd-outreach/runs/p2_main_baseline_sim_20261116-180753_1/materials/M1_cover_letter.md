Dear Ledgerly Hiring Team,

I'm applying for the UX Researcher II, AI Experiences role. For more than four years I've been a product data analyst at Cadence, a consumer fitness app. Much of that work has been about how people respond to an AI feature that makes personal decisions from their own health data. This role is the one I've been working toward: using qualitative research alongside analytics and experiments to understand how people experience AI when the stakes feel personal.

At Cadence I own the analysis for about 20 A/B tests a year across onboarding, workouts, and subscriptions, from analysis plans and power calculations through to readouts for the product team. The most relevant was the launch test for AI Coach, which adjusts workout plans using activity, sleep, and heart-rate data. In a four-week test with about 180,000 users, I found an 8% increase in weekly workouts (95% CI 6.1–9.9%) and a 2.1-point lift in 7-day retention. I checked the results week by week to rule out a novelty effect.

After launch I helped code about 1,200 open-ended responses from our in-app survey. The deck needed one sentiment number, and the final figure was 72% positive. One theme didn't fit any sentiment bucket: people who didn't understand why their plan had changed. I raised it as its own finding because it showed me that our headline metrics measured whether people used the AI, not whether they understood or trusted it. That's the gap behind your goal of building trust metrics for AI features. It's also the question I most want to work on. An unexplained change to someone's budget or savings transfer matters much more than a change to their workout plan.

I've been moving toward research on purpose. My B.S. in Information Science is from the HCI track. There I ran semi-structured interviews and affinity mapping for a research methods course, and usability-tested a prototype with eight students for my capstone. Last year I volunteered to take notes for four user interviews that Cadence's UX research team ran on AI Coach. I set up my notes for synthesis, with timestamped quotes and observations kept separate from interpretation. I haven't yet led a qualitative study end to end in industry, and that's the growth I'm looking for in this role. From day one, I can connect interview and diary-study findings to the product analytics and experiment results that persuade leadership, and I can pull the behavioral data myself in SQL.

I'd welcome the chance to talk about how I could help Ledgerly build an AI Assistant that people understand and trust with their money.

Best,
Riley Nakamura
