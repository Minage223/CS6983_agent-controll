**Subject:** Prospective PhD applicant (Fall 2027): inference from "anonymized" data

Dear Prof. Li,

I'm finishing an M.S. in Data Science at Lakeshore University and applying to the Khoury HCI PhD program for Fall 2027. I'm writing because your lab's work is close to the question I want to study, and I'd like to know if you're taking students this cycle.

My interest started on a bus ridership forecasting project. I tried a feature that combined a frequent rider's usual first boarding stop with where their phone tended to be at night. Both columns came from data described as anonymized. Together, they put a lot of riders on just a few blocks. The feature didn't help the model, so I dropped it. But the consent behind the phone data was a one-time "allow" in a partner app. Since then I keep wondering what people think can be figured out from what they share, with apps and now with AI tools they talk to. I also wonder whether they could ever actually see it.

That's why AgentDoxx and your position paper on LLM privacy across the lifecycle stood out to me. Both treat inference and re-identification as more pressing than memorization. CIDER covers the other half of what I care about: people's own boundaries for what they disclose, compared with what can actually be inferred about them.

My background is mostly quantitative. I have a BA in economics and worked for a year as a data analyst at a transit agency. I'm first author of a workshop paper on ridership forecasting at UrbanDS 2025. In a human-centered data science course, I worked on a small think-aloud study of an AutoML tool. Two of the participants assumed the tool already knew which columns were sensitive. I don't have a settled research question yet, and my training in qualitative methods is thin. That's a big part of why I want a PhD.

I've attached my CV. If you're recruiting, I'd appreciate a short conversation, or any sense of whether my interests fit the lab.

Thank you for your time.
