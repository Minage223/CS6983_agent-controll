Dear Waypoint Mobility Hiring Team,

I'm applying for the Data Scientist, Demand Forecasting role on your Forecasting team. Since 2023, most of my work has been on transit ridership data. First I prepared it for planners, and later I forecast it. Waypoint builds forecasts that planners rely on every day, and that is the work I want to keep doing.

As a research assistant in Lakeshore University's Urban Analytics Lab, I built XGBoost and LightGBM models to forecast bus ridership. They used four years of automatic passenger counter data joined with anonymized mobility data. I led the feature engineering and model evaluation, and I'm first author of the resulting paper at the UrbanDS 2025 workshop. Compared with a SARIMA seasonal baseline, our model cut MAPE on a 2023 holdout from 14.2% to 9.8%. A reviewer raised possible leakage from the mobility features, so I re-ran the analysis with a two-week lag. Error rose to 10.4% but still clearly beat the baseline, and we reported both results. I used SHAP to explain what drove the forecasts: weekday, prior-week boardings, school calendar and mobility activity. I was also explicit about the limits, since we had data from one agency and only the post-pandemic period. I'd bring that same careful approach to evaluation to your stop-, route- and network-level models.

Two semesters as a TA for a statistics course for non-majors sharpened my ability to explain technical results simply.

This summer at Northpoint Insurance, I built a gradient-boosted churn model that raised validation AUC from 0.71 to 0.76 over a logistic regression baseline.
