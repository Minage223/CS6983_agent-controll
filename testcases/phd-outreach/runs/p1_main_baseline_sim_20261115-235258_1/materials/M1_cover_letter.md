**Cover letter: Data Scientist, Demand Forecasting, Waypoint Mobility**

---

Dear Waypoint Mobility Hiring Team,

I'm applying for the Data Scientist, Demand Forecasting role on your Forecasting team. I've spent most of the last three years working with transit ridership data, first as an analyst at a regional transit agency and then as a research assistant building ridership forecasting models. I'd like to keep doing that work on a platform that planners in 40+ cities use.

At Lakeshore University's Urban Analytics Lab, I built XGBoost and LightGBM models for bus ridership. I used four years of automatic passenger counter data joined with anonymized mobility data, and I led the feature engineering and evaluation. Against a SARIMA baseline, the model cut MAPE from 14.2% to 9.8% on a 2023 holdout. A reviewer was concerned that the lagged mobility features leaked future information, so I re-ran the model with a longer lag. MAPE rose to 10.4%, which still clearly beat the baseline. That experience taught me to build evaluations that hold up under scrutiny, not just ones that produce good numbers. The work became a first-author paper at the UrbanDS 2025 workshop, and I presented it there.

Before graduate school, I was a Data Analyst at Lakeshore Regional Transit Authority. I maintained the SQL and Tableau ridership dashboards that the planning department used, and I automated the weekly reporting pipeline in Python, cutting preparation time from about six hours to one. That job showed me what planners actually need from a number: something they can trust and explain to their board. I also learned to redesign dashboards for non-technical readers.

This summer, as a Data Science Intern at Northpoint Insurance, I built a gradient-boosted churn model that raised validation AUC from 0.71 to 0.76 over the existing baseline, and I calibrated its probabilities with isotonic regression. I presented the results to the retention team and a VP in business terms, focusing on which segments the model flags and how outreach could be prioritized. The model was handed off for further evaluation. I haven't yet taken a model all the way to production, and working with your engineers to do that is one of the things that draws me to this role.

I finish my M.S. in Data Science at Lakeshore in December 2026, and I'm excited about Waypoint because the work stays close to transit. I'd welcome the chance to talk about how I could help improve your forecasts at the stop, route, and network level.

Thank you for your consideration.

Sincerely,
Morgan Reyes
