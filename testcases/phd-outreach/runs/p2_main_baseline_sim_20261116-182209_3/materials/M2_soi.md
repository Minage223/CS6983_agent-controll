Prof. Li is Tianshi Li, an assistant professor at Khoury who directs the PEACH Lab (Privacy-Enabling AI and Computer-Human Interaction). I only managed to verify her. My searches for other Khoury HCI faculty kept returning her pages, so the shortlist is her papers, sorted by how closely they match your topic.

## Read these first

1. **"Autonomy Reshapes How Personalization Affects Privacy Concerns and Trust in LLM Agents"** (COLM 2026). This is a 450-person experiment that varies how much an agent acts on its own and how it uses personal data, then measures trust, privacy concerns and willingness to use. Agents that hand control back to users when a privacy risk comes up reduced the drop in trust. It's the closest to your interest in trust in systems that act on personal data, and it uses experimental methods you already know.
2. **"Why am I seeing this: Democratizing End User Auditing for Online Content Recommendations"** (UIST 2025). It starts from the observation that people form hypotheses about why personalized content appears but have no way to check them. That's very close to your "didn't understand why the plan changed" theme.
3. **"It's a Fair Game, or Is It?"** (CHI 2024). It combines an analysis of real ChatGPT conversations with 19 interviews and finds that users' wrong mental models limited their awareness of privacy risks. It's mixed methods and centered on mental models, the pairing your statement proposes.

## Also worth skimming

4. **"Beyond Permissions: Investigating Mobile Personalization with Simulated Personas"** (HAIPS 2025 workshop). It's a toolkit for auditing how apps adapt to sensor data and found measurable adaptation in fitness apps. This connects directly to AI Coach.
5. **"Alert now or never"** (TOCHI 2022). A one-week experience-sampling study plus a model personalized from user actions. It pairs behavioral data with self-reports, as you want to.
6. **"Secret Use of Large Language Models"** (CSCW 2025). A survey of 125 real cases plus an experiment with 300 people. It's another example of her mixed-methods work.

## What this means for your statement

Her core area is privacy, and your draft barely mentions it. The overlap is real, though: personalization from personal data, transparency, and trust in systems that act for the user.
